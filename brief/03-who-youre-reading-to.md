# Who you are reading to

*Paste this when the output is for somebody other than you. The audience changes
the argument far more than it changes the numbers.*

---

## 1. The distributor — the buyer

**Who:** 45–60, runs a ₹2–20 crore business, second generation. Books kept in
Excel by a son or a nephew. Fluent in his trade, not in software. Has been sold
an ERP before and either refused it or abandoned it.

**What he actually worries about:** money stuck in stock that isn't moving,
customers who owe him and have gone quiet, whether Friday's payment will clear,
and whether the boy at the second branch is actually selling.

**What wins him:** *"Send me your file. Don't change anything."* Then show him
three things he did not know about his own business.

**What loses him instantly:** the word "platform". Login screens. Anything that
implies he has to enter his last two years of history before it works. Monthly
per-user pricing. Being told his current system is wrong.

**Write for him:** short sentences. Rupees in figures. Concrete nouns — bags,
godown, bill, party. Never "leverage", "streamline", "solution", "empower".

---

## 2. The prospect's accountant — the gatekeeper

**Who:** the CA or the clerk who will be asked "is this alright?"

**What he cares about:** whether the invoice is a valid tax invoice, whether the
GST split is right, whether the numbers reconcile with what he already has, and
whether he now has more work.

**What wins him:** invoice numbering that is sequential and gap-free. CGST/SGST
against IGST handled correctly. Statements that say what they are missing rather
than quietly omitting it. The fact that Vyuha reads the existing file rather
than asking him to re-enter anything.

**What loses him:** a number that disagrees with his by a rupee. Anything that
looks like it is trying to replace him.

---

## 3. An investor

**What he cares about:** how big the problem is, why now, why this team, and
whether the thing is actually built or is a deck.

**The argument that works:**
- Millions of Indian distributors run on Excel and WhatsApp. Existing software
  demands they change first, which is why adoption is low.
- Vyuha inverts it: read what they already have. Zero migration.
- It is built, not planned — the engine reads real messy files today, and there
  is a working product behind it.
- The wedge is the dashboard; the retention is the daily console; the expansion
  is invoicing and orders.

**What loses him:** an addressable-market slide with no evidence behind it.
Claiming features that are not built. Vague traction.

---

## 4. A bank or lender — for the client, not for Vyuha

**What they care about:** can the borrower repay. In order: cash conversion
cycle, receivables ageing, current ratio, revenue trend, customer concentration.

**The strongest line in this data:** the **negative 25-day cash cycle**. The
business collects before it pays. That is a lender's favourite number and most
distributors cannot produce it.

**Be careful:** the balance sheet is partial — no opening balances, no fixed
assets, no loans. Say so in the document. A statement that omits liabilities
without flagging it is worse than no statement.
