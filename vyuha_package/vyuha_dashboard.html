Challenges & proposed solutions
Data trust barrier
Distributors are protective of buyer lists, pricing, and margin data. Handing Excel files to a stranger feels risky.
Proposed solution
Start with someone you know personally or who was referred. Sign a simple NDA/data agreement upfront. Show that you process data locally and never share it. First client = trust demonstration for the next.
Dirty Excel data
Real Excel files have merged cells, inconsistent naming, broken formulas, missing entries. Cleaning takes invisible time.
Proposed solution
Build a data cleaning script as part of the base layer. Charge a small "data audit fee" upfront. Set expectation: "We'll need 2–3 days to clean before building." Makes the invisible work visible and paid.
Scope creep
Clients will keep adding requests once you're inside their business. Without clear boundaries, one client becomes a full-time job.
Proposed solution
One-page scope document before every project. List exactly what's included and what's not. Extra requests = new quote. Framing this as "professional process" actually builds more trust.
Explaining AI value to non-tech owners
A 55-year-old distributor doesn't know what a dashboard is. Abstract value doesn't sell.
Proposed solution
Show before pitching. Build a sample dashboard from dummy data that looks like their business. Walk in with a live demo, not a slide deck. "This is what your weekly report will look like" closes faster than any explanation.
Pricing resistance
₹15,000–₹50,000 is not small for a distributor. "Why can't my nephew do this in Excel?" is a real objection.
Proposed solution
Lead with time saved, not technology. "This saves your owner 3 hours a day = 90 hours a month. What's that worth?" Frame the ROI in their language. Offer a ₹2,999 pilot report first to remove risk.
Scaling is hard early
Every client has different data structures. The automation for ABC Gears won't copy directly to XYZ Fasteners.
Proposed solution
This is exactly why the platform model (base layer + custom config) matters. After 5–6 clients you'll see the patterns repeat. Document every client engagement — that pattern library becomes your competitive moat.
